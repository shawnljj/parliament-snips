Reading mode — three directions

Every direction uses the same real content: the sitting of 7 February 2024 — 23 briefs, 91,757 words, 387 turns, 55 reports. Including the motion with 202 sections and a written answer of 54 words. Nothing invented.

Each is a complete design system — type, colour, density, motion posture, interaction model — with the same three screens: home (search above the sitting), read (summary into transcript), answer (a cited answer that lands in the passage).

1 · Margin

Editorial serif · the transcript is the page

The sitting as a printed document you annotate. The summary is a marginal note; the transcript is the body. Most reading comfort, most honest about what the record says — and the slowest.

2 · Index

Dense sans · the sitting as a catalogue

The sitting as a dataset you navigate: numbered items, word counts as a column, sections as a scannable index. Fastest to find something; coldest to read.

Open direction 2 ›

3 · Fold

Progressive disclosure · one column, opens where you tap

The sitting as a stack of closed briefs. Big tap targets, nothing hidden behind a second page, sections open in place. The most phone-native.

Shared with all three

  • Search above the sitting (your call)
  • Refusal shown as a result, not an error
  • Answer → "read this in the sitting" → lands in the passage
  • Sentence highlight uses the same yellow as the shipped site
  • 0 horizontal overflow at 360/390px

Deliberately not done

  • No hero image, no centred stack
  • No feature tiles or icon toppers
  • No gradient, no glassmorphism
  • No invented numbers or placeholder copy
  • No party colours anywhere

Measurements to check

  • Screens tall at 390px for the home view
  • Minimum tap target ≥44px
  • Text contrast on the amber highlight
  • Whether 26 sections is the right cap
The one structural finding: brief length is wildly bimodal. On this sitting, two motions carry 202 and 152 sections while twenty written answers carry 1–6. A flat list of briefs hides that; a design that treats all 23 the same will either drown the reader in the two big ones or undersell them. Every direction signals weight — differently. That's the main thing to judge.
What I need from you:
  1. Which direction to build (or which parts to take from more than one).
  2. Whether the summary should sit above its transcript or be folded behind it — this is the biggest reading difference between Margin and Fold.
  3. Whether 26 sections is the right visible cap before "open the full transcript".
